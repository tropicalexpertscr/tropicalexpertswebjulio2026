How much does a private transfer cost in Costa Rica? The real 2026 prices

Searching for transfer prices in Costa Rica is exhausting: half the websites hide the fare behind a form and the other half quote it without taxes or surcharges. Here is ours in full, with what it includes and what it does not — plus the questions we suggest you ask any transport company, ourselves included.

Straight to it, since that is why you came: these are private transfer prices per vehicle — not per person — on our most requested routes, taxes already included. One vehicle for up to 5 passengers; for groups of up to 9 or 12, the site's calculator shows all three prices for every route.

What a serious price includes

In a formal transfer, the per-vehicle fare should cover: flight tracking and waiting if you land late, a named sign at arrivals, child seats at no cost, liability insurance, air conditioning, and all taxes, with an electronic invoice. When a price looks below market, it is almost always because something on this list is charged separately — or does not exist.

What raises the price, with numbers

Three things, all optional. Vehicle size: the 9-seater and the 12-seater cost more than the 5-passenger minivan. Extra touring hours: $35 per hour, if you want to turn the transfer into a day of lookouts and waterfalls. And the VIP tier ($80), which adds one to two hours of sightseeing stops with a welcome kit. Nothing else: no luggage fees, no child fees, no «fuel surcharge», no surprises at payment.

The three questions before booking with anyone

«Is the price final, tax and invoice included?» If they hesitate, walk away. The electronic invoice is not a luxury: it is your recourse if something goes wrong.

«What happens if my flight is delayed?» The correct answer is «we wait at no charge — that is why we ask for your flight number». A company that charges to wait out an airline delay is billing you for its own job.

«Are you registered with the ICT?» Formal tourist transport in Costa Rica operates under a license from the Costa Rican Tourism Institute, with insurance in force. Ours is #3946-2025 and sits at the foot of every page on this site. Ask for that number from anyone about to drive your family across a mountain.

The short version

Private transfers in Costa Rica are priced per vehicle, not per person: the bigger the group, the cheaper per head. A serious price is final — taxes, seats, waiting and insurance included — and stands behind a verifiable ICT license. Everything else is fine print.
